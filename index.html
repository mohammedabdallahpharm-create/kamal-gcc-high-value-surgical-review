Executive summary

What the initial research indicates — and what it does not yet prove.

Evidence-backed observations
  • Dubai has a specific DHA framework addressing virtual second opinions.
  • Abu Dhabi and federal rules distinguish healthcare delivery, insurance administration and health-data handling.
  • Second-opinion services already exist in the UAE through provider, insurer-linked and independent models.
Open questions
  • Which exact licence or approval applies to Kamal GCC's final activities?
  • Will insurers / TPAs procure a specialist surgical review service separately from existing case management?
  • Is there a demonstrable unmet need in complex, high-cost surgical cases?
Preliminary positioning to test: an insurer-focused coordination and access layer for independent specialist review of selected complex or high-value surgical cases. This is a market hypothesis, not a validated competitive advantage.

01 · Regulatory landscape

The regulatory classification depends on what the entity actually does, where the patient and specialist are located, and how clinical records are handled.

Dubai · DHA

Virtual second opinions

  • DHA's Telehealth Services Standard, Version 4, expressly addresses virtual second opinions for complex or specialised cases.
  • The standard describes licensed specialists / consultants and an advisory report model, with the treating physician retaining clinical responsibility.
  • This is a Dubai-specific framework; do not assume it applies identically across the UAE.
Read DHA standard ↗
Abu Dhabi · DoH

TPA and provider boundaries

  • DoH health-insurance legislation regulates the role of TPAs and their relationship with insurers.
  • TPA status should not be assumed merely because a business coordinates a medical opinion.
  • Provider licensing and professional scope remain relevant where clinical services are delivered.
Read DoH Book 2 ↗
Federal · CBUAE

Insurance administration

  • CBUAE's TPA instructions describe regulated insurance-administration activities, including claims-related functions.
  • The precise activity mix must be compared against current licensing requirements before selecting a business classification.
Read CBUAE instructions ↗
Federal · Health data

Confidentiality and data handling

  • Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 regulates ICT use in the health sector, including confidentiality and restrictions relevant to processing health data outside the UAE.
  • Data access, patient consent / lawful basis, storage location and permitted disclosures require specific review.
Read Federal Law No. 2 of 2019 ↗
Regulatory caution: This is an initial research summary, not legal advice or a final licensing opinion. The federal and emirate-level position must be checked against current instruments and the final operating model. Coverage of all seven emirates is not yet complete.

02 · Possible insurer / TPA workflow

Illustrative service flow for discussion. It is not presented as an approved or standard market process.

STEP 01Insurer / TPA

Identifies a case and defines the clinical question for independent review.

STEP 02Kamal GCC

Coordinates intake, document completeness, permissions and reviewer matching.

STEP 03Independent specialist

Reviews the case and provides an opinion within their licensed scope.

STEP 04Kamal GCC

Coordinates secure report delivery and records the workflow.

STEP 05Insurer / TPA

Considers the report and retains its own coverage / claims decision.

Keep these functions distinct

  • Clinical second opinion: independent clinical view on diagnosis or proposed treatment.
  • Medical necessity review: assessment of whether a service is medically justified.
  • Coverage decision: insurer's determination under policy terms and applicable rules.

Questions for buyer validation

  • Who commissions and pays for the review — insurer or TPA?
  • What question must the specialist answer?
  • Is a written report sufficient, or is a case conference needed?
  • What turnaround time, data route and escalation process are expected?

Competitive conclusion: Second opinions are not a new or empty category in the UAE. The specific proposition — independent, insurer-focused review of selected high-value surgical cases — remains to be tested against direct providers, in-house insurer / TPA capabilities and procurement evidence. Not finding a public offer is not proof that no such service exists.
