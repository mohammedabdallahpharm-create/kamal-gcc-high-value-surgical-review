# Evidence and Source Verification Standards

## 1. Required source fields

Every source record should capture, where available:

- Source ID
- Publisher / responsible entity
- Exact document or dataset title
- Direct URL
- Source category
- Jurisdiction / emirate
- Publication or release date
- Coverage period
- Access date
- Source type (law, regulation, dataset, annual report, tender, policy, study, company page, news, etc.)
- Inspection status
- Relevant extracted finding
- Limitations and caveats
- Related research question
- Confidence assessment

## 2. Inspection statuses

- INSPECTED: the source page or document was opened and relevant content reviewed.
- SNIPPET_ONLY: only a search-result excerpt was reviewed.
- LINK_ONLY: a URL was identified but its content was not reviewed.
- INACCESSIBLE: access failed or the content could not be retrieved.
- NOT_YET_CHECKED: identified as a lead, not yet inspected.

A search snippet is not equivalent to an inspected source.

## 3. Evidence classes

- Primary evidence: legislation, regulator publications, official datasets, procurement records, audited reports, official policies, and direct institutional disclosures.
- Secondary evidence: reputable journalism, independent research, academic analysis, and industry reports.
- Tertiary leads: search snippets, directories, aggregators, and unverified summaries.

## 4. Claim labels

- FACT: directly supported by inspected evidence.
- REPORTED: attributed statement from a source, not independently established.
- INFERENCE: reasoned interpretation based on cited facts.
- HYPOTHESIS: proposition requiring testing.
- UNKNOWN: insufficient accessible evidence.
- CONTRADICTED: credible evidence conflicts with the claim.

## 5. Rules for quantitative claims

- Preserve the original unit, denominator, geography, and time period.
- Distinguish procedures, admissions, claims, approvals, and unique patients.
- Distinguish billed charges, allowed amounts, paid amounts, and total episode costs.
- Do not extrapolate a market size from unrelated activity metrics.
- Record whether values are actual, estimated, forecast, or illustrative.
- Never describe a figure as UAE-wide unless the source supports UAE-wide coverage.

## 6. Research integrity

- Record contrary evidence and alternative explanations.
- Keep source wording separate from analyst interpretation.
- Do not infer that a service or provider does not exist merely because it was not found.
- Do not use confidential or patient-identifiable information.
- Recheck laws, circulars, licensing requirements, and operational policies before relying on them.
