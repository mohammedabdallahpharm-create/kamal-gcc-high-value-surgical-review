# Kamal GCC — Platform Concept (Working Hypothesis)

## Purpose

Explore a structured B2B connection between health insurers / TPAs and independent medical specialists for complex, high-value surgical cases.

## Potential workflow

1. **Payer request:** insurer or TPA identifies a case for an independent specialist opinion under its internal policies and applicable law.
2. **Case intake and scope:** request, clinical question, required specialty, deadlines and permitted records are defined.
3. **Specialist matching:** a suitable specialist is identified using verified qualifications, licence status, specialty scope, availability and conflict checks.
4. **Secure review:** records are made available through an approved, secure workflow after required legal basis, authorisations and safeguards are confirmed.
5. **Independent opinion:** the specialist provides a documented professional opinion within their competence and applicable licence.
6. **Payer decision:** insurer / TPA retains its own coverage, benefits and claims decision responsibilities.
7. **Audit trail:** request, access, disclosures, reviewer identity, opinion delivery and decision hand-off are recorded.

## Proposed role boundaries

- Kamal GCC is being explored as a coordination and specialist-network layer, not assumed to be the clinical provider or payer decision-maker.
- Specialists must act within their professional licence, competence and independence requirements.
- Treating clinicians retain their responsibilities under applicable law and clinical standards.
- Insurers / TPAs retain their contractual and regulatory obligations.
- Whether coordination, matching, case handling or subcontracting itself requires a particular licence remains an open regulatory question.

## Questions to validate

- Who is the contracting customer: insurer, TPA, or both?
- Who commissions and pays for each opinion?
- Who owns specialist credentialing and ongoing quality assurance?
- What clinical question is being answered: diagnosis, surgical indication, medical necessity, proposed approach, alternatives, or expected resource use?
- Does the specialist communicate only with the payer, or also with the treating physician / patient?
- What is the boundary between an advisory opinion and a delegated claims decision?
- What facility, professional, TPA, data and outsourcing permissions are required in each emirate?
- How are conflicts of interest, confidentiality, liability, audit and complaints handled?

## Design guardrails

- No patient data in this public repository.
- No clinical recommendation or payer decision is automated by the concept document.
- No claim of regulatory approval, licensing or market-wide adoption.
- No assumption that AED 500,000 is a market or regulatory standard.
- No public claim that the service is available until licensing, contracting, clinical governance and data controls are confirmed.
